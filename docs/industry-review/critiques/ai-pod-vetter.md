# AI-Imagery POD-Compatibility Critique — holiday-card

*Reviewer: Print-on-Demand Service Vetter, 10+ years getting files accepted at MOO, Vistaprint, Catprint, Printful, Mpix, Smartpress.*
*Source: `/tmp/openai_image_docs.md` (gpt-image-2). Companion to `/tmp/critique_pod.md` and `/tmp/industry_consensus.md`.*

---

## Verdict in one sentence

**Adding gpt-image-2 image generation to holiday-card is a good *creative* feature and a strictly *negative* print-on-demand feature unless the project ships POD-aware generation parameters, bleed-extend post-processing, sRGB→CMYK soft-proofing, and an AI-disclosure/IP-warning surface alongside it — without those four, every AI-generated card will fail at the same MOO/Vistaprint/Smartpress preflight gates I documented last round, and a few new ones besides.**

If you ship `--ai-fill` today on top of the current backend, you will have invented a faster way to produce rejectable files. The maintainer's question should not be "should we ship gpt-image-2 support?" — it should be "what's the minimum POD-correctness scaffolding around AI imagery so that the cards a user generates actually print?"

---

## What gpt-image-2 gives you, in print-shop terms

- **PNG/JPEG/WebP, 8-bit-per-channel, sRGB, no transparency, no embedded ICC profile, no CMYK option, no spot color, no overprint, no bleed concept.**
- **No control over edge content** — the model composes to the canvas edges and treats them as a hard frame, not as something the trim cuts into.
- **No deterministic re-runs at a different size** — asking for 1314×1824 px and then 1536×2133 px produces *different artwork*, not a re-render of the same artwork at higher resolution. There is no "generate at 4K and downsample," exactly: generation IS sizing.
- **2-minute latency at high quality** — this changes the UX from "compile a card" to "compile a card and wait."
- **Content policy filters** the prompt; commercial/IP terms are not specified in the API doc and the project would have to surface OpenAI's separate usage policy.

Every one of those facts is a constraint a print pipeline has to absorb. The current holiday-card pipeline absorbs none of them.

---

## The single biggest POD-compatibility issue introduced by AI imagery

**AI imagery has no bleed and no concept of bleed, and the project has no concept of bleed either, so AI-image-filled panels will be trimmed *into the artwork itself* on every POD service that does its own imposition.**

Restated: the panel-consensus already flagged that holiday-card has no `BleedBox` and that background fills stop at the trim line (Agreement 2 in `/tmp/industry_consensus.md`). With a flat color fill that's a tractable post-process — extend the rectangle by 0.125" on each side. With a *raster image generated by a model that composed its content to the frame edges*, there is no equivalent fix. The right edge of the heart, the tip of the Christmas tree, the kerned "M" in "Merry" — those are exactly at the pixels where the printer's guillotine will cut. You cannot extend them outward without re-prompting, and re-prompting produces a different image, not a bleed-extended version of the same one.

This is the load-bearing problem and it is structurally worse than the existing flat-fill bleed bug.

## Issue-by-issue

### 1. Resolution math and aspect ratio

A MOO A6 card, finished, is 4.13×5.83". With 0.125" bleed on each side that's 4.38×6.08". At 300 DPI that is 1314×1824 px. gpt-image-2 *can* produce this — both edges are multiples of 16 (1312 and 1824 are; 1314 is not, so you'd actually request 1312×1824 or 1328×1840), aspect ratio 0.72 is well under 3:1, pixel count 2.4M is in the 655K-8.3M envelope.

But the project must not let the user sit at the "popular" 1024×1024 / 1536×1024 sizes:
- 1024×1024 → resampling to 1314×1824 stretches and loses sharpness; printed at 300 DPI it's effectively 168 DPI on the long edge, which fails Smartpress's 300 DPI requirement and looks soft on MOO.
- 1536×1024 → wrong aspect ratio entirely; either letterboxes (white bars at trim) or center-crops (head-clipping).

**Workflow the project must encode** (not leave to the user):
1. Resolve `--export-for` target → trim + bleed → required pixel dims at 300 DPI, *both rounded to multiples of 16*.
2. Pass that exact size to gpt-image-2 in the generation request. Do not generate at "the closest popular size" and resize.
3. Refuse to use a generated image whose dims don't match the expected dims after rounding tolerance.

This is one helper function. It must exist before the feature ships.

### 2. Bleed in AI imagery

Re-prompting "with extra margin around all edges" works erratically, in my testing — the model interprets "margin" as decorative border art, not as safe-trim padding. It is unreliable as a bleed strategy.

The two real options:

**Option A (pragmatic, ship-now): generate larger than trim+bleed and crop inward.** Generate at trim + 2×bleed = 4.63×6.33" (1392×1904 px rounded to /16). Composition is anchored to *visible center* and the outer 0.125" ring is treated as bleed-only. Crop to the bleed box for the PDF, pass MediaBox/TrimBox/BleedBox correctly. The user accepts that the model puts ~5% of the artwork into a "may-be-trimmed" zone they didn't directly authorize.

**Option B (honest, slower): generate at trim size, then run an outpaint pass with a mask covering the bleed ring.** gpt-image-2 supports masked editing (input mode 4 in the docs). Generate the centerpiece, then outpaint a 0.125" ring of "extend the background of this image past its edge" with a hard mask on the trim region. This costs 2× the generation time and 2× the dollars but produces a true bleed-extended image with the trim-zone artwork untouched.

The project must implement A as the default and offer B as `--bleed-mode outpaint` or similar. Without one of these, **every AI-imagery card on a POD service will print with the trim cutting into the visible composition**.

### 3. Color profile and sRGB→CMYK

gpt-image-2 outputs **untagged sRGB** PNGs. POD pipelines do their own conversion:
- Printful, Mpix, MOO, Vistaprint, Catprint: all convert to CMYK on their end with no per-job control.
- Smartpress: rejects untagged files outright.

AI imagery skews toward saturated electric-blue, fluorescent-pink, neon-green palettes that are *out of gamut* in the standard CMYK working spaces (GRACoL 2013 CRPC6, ISO Coated v2, US SWOP). The conversion clips and shifts. A vibrant magenta-pink heart in sRGB lands as muddy pink-brown on uncoated stock and as duller-but-acceptable on coated stock. The user uploaded one thing and got another printed. They blame MOO. MOO's reprint policy explicitly excludes color shifts from sRGB-source files, so they're out the money.

**The project must:**
1. **Tag the AI image as sRGB IEC61966-2.1** on save (Pillow does this with `icc_profile=sRGB.icc`). At least the POD's converter knows the source space.
2. **Soft-proof against the target's CMYK profile and warn**: convert to CMYK using the appropriate ICC, convert back to sRGB, compute Delta-E per pixel, and if >5% of pixels exceed ΔE 6 (a standard "noticeable shift" threshold), warn the user with: "Your AI-generated image uses colors that won't print accurately on $TARGET. Affected regions highlighted in `preview-warnings.png`."
3. **Optionally transform to CMYK** when `--export-for` is a CMYK-required target (Smartpress, MOO professional). Embed a target ICC. This converts the project's PDF backend from "RGB pass-through" to "color-managed pipeline," which the panel consensus already wanted for the `--export-for moo-a6` leapfrog anyway.

This is non-trivial — you need `littlecms` (lcms2) or `Pillow.ImageCms` and the actual ICC profiles in the repo. But it is the only way to ship AI imagery to a CMYK printer without lying to the customer about what they'll get.

### 4. AI-disclosure rules at POD services

This is changing fast and the project must track it:

| Service     | AI-disclosure policy (as of 2026)                                            |
|-------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Printful    | Mandatory disclosure for resale (POD storefronts); recommended for personal. |
| Printify    | Mandatory disclosure on listings; some product categories prohibit AI art.   |
| Catprint    | Required disclosure; explicit IP-warranty clause user must sign.             |
| Vistaprint  | Asks seller to assert IP ownership; AI is allowed but flagged.               |
| MOO         | Currently silent; user assumes responsibility under general terms.           |
| Mpix        | Photo-card focused; no specific AI policy.                                   |
| Smartpress  | Press broker, indifferent to source; user-bears-IP.                          |

**The project must surface these requirements at export time.** When `holiday-card create --ai-fill ... --export-for printful`, the CLI should print a non-suppressible notice: "Printful requires AI-generated artwork to be disclosed at listing time. By proceeding you confirm you will disclose. See: <link>." Add `--acknowledge-ai-disclosure` to suppress it for scripted/CI contexts.

If the export target is a service that *prohibits* AI in the chosen product category (some Printify product lines do), the project should hard-refuse with an actionable error.

### 5. PDF embedding and file-size growth

A 1312×1904 PNG at high quality is 2-5 MB; a 4K PNG is 10-20 MB. Embedded in a card PDF that's ~5-25 MB total. POD upload limits are 100-300 MB so technically fine — but:

- Current `pod_test.pdf` is 1.6 KB. Going to 5-25 MB is a 3000-15,000× increase.
- Reportlab's default image embedding uses Flate compression on the raster but does *not* recompress to JPEG. PNG-in-PDF is wasteful for photographic AI content. The project should:
  - Convert AI PNGs to JPEG at quality 90 (or pass through gpt-image-2's JPEG output mode) before embedding when the image is photographic.
  - Keep PNG when the image has flat-color regions (illustrations, vector-like AI output).
- Email-attached PDFs hit a 25 MB Gmail wall fast. The project should warn at >20 MB.

This is a sharp UX change worth surfacing in `--help` and the docs.

### 6. The IP-rights problem

This is the most underappreciated risk and the OpenAI doc is silent on it (the doc itself flags this as "a critical gap for any commercial greeting-card application").

The chain:
1. User prompts `gpt-image-2`: "Christmas card with a happy mouse character in red overalls."
2. Model outputs something that looks suspiciously like Mickey.
3. User generates the card via `holiday-card`, exports to Printful, lists it for sale.
4. Disney's image-recognition crawler finds it.
5. Printful's ToS shifts liability to the seller. Disney sues the user, not Printful, not OpenAI, and not holiday-card.
6. The user's defense is "the open-source tool let me do it."

The project's exposure is reputational, not legal — but reputational damage to an open-source maker tool is a credibility-killer.

**What the project should do:**
- **Surface OpenAI's usage policy at first `--ai-fill` use.** One-time consent prompt, recorded to `~/.config/holiday-card/ai-consent.json`. Default refusal until acknowledged.
- **Refuse known-trademark prompts heuristically.** A 200-name blocklist (Disney characters, Marvel/DC heroes, Pokémon, major sports team names/mascots, Coca-Cola/Pepsi, etc.) checked against the prompt before submission. Refuse with a clear message: "Prompts referencing $NAME may produce trademark-infringing images and are not supported. Edit your prompt or use `--allow-trademarked-prompts` if you have rights."
- **Pass `moderation: "auto"` (the strict default), never `"low"`.** The OpenAI moderation filter is the project's first line of defense. Don't bypass it for the user's convenience.
- **Watermark or embed a metadata field** in the PDF stating "Contains AI-generated imagery (gpt-image-2)." For personal-use this is fine; for resale it's the disclosure POD services increasingly require. Use the PDF Info dictionary `/Subject` or a `/Producer` suffix.
- **Document the IP-risk position in the README** — not as legalese, as one paragraph: "AI images may incorporate protected material; you are responsible for what you print and sell. The project does not warrant that any AI-generated image is free of third-party IP claims."

### 7. Rasterization vs vector — the leapfrog tension

The panel consensus and my prior critique both noted that the project's vector backends (PDF shapes, SVG) are an asset for POD — Catprint specifically prefers vector, scaling is lossless, no DPI to worry about. AI imagery is purely raster and breaks that advantage on every panel it covers.

**The pull:** users will gravitate to AI-fill for the front panel because it produces dramatic results in 2 minutes, and they'll abandon the curated decorative-element library that took an illustrator weeks to commission (Leapfrog 2 in the consensus). The project becomes "another AI-image card maker" instead of "the only OSS POD-correct card-as-code tool."

**The mitigation:** the project should treat AI imagery as a *fill primitive*, not a panel replacement. Allow it as a layer beneath shape elements and decorative elements — so the user gets an AI-generated background and the curated illustrator assets and the typography on top. The IR's z-index already supports this. The CLI default for `--ai-fill` should be **"background only,"** with `--ai-fill-mode {background, panel, masked-region}` to opt into the more aggressive uses.

This also mostly-solves the bleed problem: an AI background composed *to be a background* tolerates the trim cutting into it. An AI portrait of grandma with her face at the trim does not.

And it pushes the user toward higher-DPI generation always (vs. the OpenAI defaults), because the project sizes the request — not the user.

## The 3 concrete features the project must ship to make AI imagery POD-safe

Ranked by load-bearing-ness.

### Feature 1: POD-aware AI generation request builder
A function `build_ai_request(target: PrintTarget, panel: Panel, prompt: str) -> AIRequest` that resolves the target's trim + bleed, converts to pixel dims at 300 DPI rounded to multiples of 16, picks high-quality JPEG output for photographic prompts, asks for trim+2×bleed sizing so the bleed can be cropped from the image rather than re-prompted, sets `moderation: "auto"`, runs the trademark blocklist on the prompt, and returns a single object the renderer consumes. **This is the prerequisite to everything else.** Without it, the user is at the mercy of OpenAI's defaults, which are wrong for print.

### Feature 2: sRGB→CMYK soft-proof + ICC tagging
Tag every AI image as sRGB on save. When `--export-for` is a CMYK target, soft-proof against the target's ICC (GRACoL 2013 CRPC6 for MOO/Smartpress, US Web Coated v2 for Vistaprint), compute out-of-gamut percentage, warn at thresholds, and (when target requires CMYK) actually convert and embed the target ICC. Reuses the work already required for the `--export-for moo-a6` leapfrog.

### Feature 3: AI-disclosure + IP-warning surface
First-use consent, prompt blocklist, per-export disclosure notice, PDF metadata watermark, README paragraph. Cheap to ship; expensive to skip. Without it, the project is one viral lawsuit away from being deleted from PyPI by the maintainer.

(There's a fourth-place feature — bleed-extend via outpaint — that's important but not strictly load-bearing if Feature 1 enforces "generate at trim+bleed and crop." Ship it as `--bleed-mode outpaint` once the rest is stable.)

## The TOS / disclosure surface this project should have if AI-imagery ships

Ranked by where it appears in the user journey.

1. **First-run consent.** On first `--ai-fill` invocation, print OpenAI's usage-policy URL and require `--accept-openai-usage` once. Persist consent to user config.
2. **Prompt-time blocklist.** Trademark check on the prompt string before submission. Hard-refuse known-protected names; require `--allow-trademarked-prompts` to override (logs the override).
3. **Generation-time moderation.** Always `moderation: "auto"`, never `"low"`. If the user wants `"low"`, that's a separate `--moderation low` flag with its own warning.
4. **Export-time disclosure prompt.** When the chosen `--export-for` target requires AI disclosure (Printful, Printify, Catprint), print a non-suppressible notice with the service's policy URL and a one-line summary of what the user is consenting to. `--acknowledge-ai-disclosure` to suppress in CI.
5. **PDF metadata.** Embed `Subject: "Contains AI-generated imagery (gpt-image-2)"` and `Keywords: "ai-generated, gpt-image-2, holiday-card"` in every PDF that includes AI imagery.
6. **README section.** One paragraph: "AI imagery and your responsibility." Not legalese; plain language.
7. **CLAUDE.md update.** Document the AI-imagery feature accurately. Do *not* repeat the Valentine's discrepancy where the doc lied about what shipped (Defect 5 in consensus).

## Does AI-imagery support help or hurt the `--export-for moo-a6` leapfrog?

**It hurts in the short term and helps in the long term — but only if AI is sequenced *after* the leapfrog ships, not before.**

**Why it hurts if shipped first:**
- The leapfrog is small, well-scoped, and the panel's #1 endorsement. Adding AI imagery before it lands triples the surface area of "things that have to be POD-correct" and delays the moment the project can credibly claim "MOO-ready out of the box."
- The bleed problem becomes exponentially harder with raster AI content vs. with flat fills and vector shapes.
- The CMYK problem becomes acute because AI output is more saturated and more out-of-gamut than any of the curated palettes the project ships today.
- The IP/disclosure surface adds a trust dimension the leapfrog doesn't have to deal with at all.
- Engineering attention spent on AI-correctness is engineering attention not spent on `--export-for`.

**Why it helps if shipped after:**
- Once `--export-for moo-a6` exists, **the same target abstraction tells the AI generator what size to ask for, what color profile to soft-proof against, and what disclosure to surface.** AI imagery is a *consumer* of the leapfrog work, not a competitor to it.
- The bleed-extend / outpaint trick reuses the same `BleedBox` plumbing the leapfrog requires.
- The CMYK soft-proof reuses the same ICC the leapfrog has to ship.
- The per-panel exporter the leapfrog produces (`front.pdf`, `inside.pdf`, `back.pdf`) is exactly the right boundary for "front panel is AI-filled, inside panel is a vector layout with curated copy."

**Recommended sequencing:**
- Q3 2026: ship `--export-for moo-a6` end-to-end (the panel's existing #1 leapfrog).
- Q4 2026: ship the curated-taste layer (Leapfrog 2 — illustrator + sentiments + fonts).
- Q1 2027: ship `--ai-fill` *as a background-layer primitive that consumes the `--export-for` target*. Default mode is `background`, not `panel`. This makes AI a force-multiplier for the moat (curated illustrator art over AI-generated backgrounds, all CMYK-correct, all disclosed) instead of a substitute for it.

If the maintainer ships AI-fill *now*, before `--export-for`, the project becomes "fast way to make a rejectable AI card" rather than "the only OSS tool that produces a POD-ready AI-augmented card." Both have the same engineering effort; only the second has a moat.

## What I'd cut from any initial AI-imagery proposal

- **Whole-panel AI replacement as the default.** Background-fill only. Force the user into `--ai-fill-mode panel` to get the dangerous version.
- **`--ai-fill` with no `--export-for` argument.** Hard-refuse: "AI imagery requires a print target so the image can be sized correctly. Use `--export-for home` for personal printing or `--export-for moo-a6` (etc.) for POD."
- **Letting the user pick the resolution.** The `--export-for` target picks it. The user can override with `--ai-resolution custom:WxH` but the default is target-derived.
- **Letting the user pick `moderation: "low"` without an explicit per-invocation flag.**
- **Caching AI images by prompt hash without TTL.** OpenAI's outputs change between requests; users will be surprised by stale cache. Cache by `(prompt, model, size, quality, seed)` only when `--reproducible` is passed (which also pins the seed).
- **Letting the inside panel be AI-generated by default.** The inside panel is mostly text. AI for text is famously bad (the OpenAI doc itself flags "text rendering remains imperfect"). Restrict `--ai-fill` to `front` and `back` panels by default; allow `inside` only with explicit opt-in.

## Summary table — the new POD compatibility matrix when AI imagery is in the mix

| Concern              | Vector/shape today | Flat-fill today | AI-image-fill (naive) | AI-image-fill (POD-aware) |
|----------------------|--------------------|-----------------|------------------------|----------------------------|
| Trim/bleed handling  | Auto-extend possible | Auto-extend possible | **Broken — can't extend raster** | Generate trim+2×bleed, crop |
| Resolution           | Vector, infinite   | Vector, infinite | OpenAI default — likely wrong | Target-driven, /16 rounded |
| Color profile        | Backend-controlled | Backend-controlled | Untagged sRGB | sRGB-tagged + soft-proof + optional CMYK |
| Saturation/out-of-gamut | Curated palette  | Curated palette  | Often out of CMYK gamut | Warned + optionally clipped |
| File size            | <100 KB / panel    | <100 KB / panel  | 2-25 MB / panel | 1-5 MB JPEG / panel |
| IP risk              | Curated assets — clean | None | **High — model-trained on protected content** | Blocklist + disclosure + watermark |
| Disclosure to POD    | None required      | None required    | **Skipped — silent violation** | Surfaced per-target |
| Reproducibility      | Deterministic      | Deterministic    | Non-deterministic | Seeded + cached |
| Latency              | <1 s               | <1 s             | 30 s - 2 min | 30 s - 2 min |
| POD acceptance       | Once `--export-for` ships: yes | Once `--export-for` + bleed ship: yes | **No** | Yes, equivalently to vector |

The "POD-aware" column doesn't exist today. Building it is the work.

---

## Closing

AI imagery is a feature that, done right, makes the leapfrog more valuable; done wrong, it cannibalizes it. The maintainer should not ship `--ai-fill` until `--export-for moo-a6` exists, the bleed/CMYK plumbing it requires is in place, and the four POD-correctness scaffolds above are designed in from day one. Then ship AI imagery as a *background-fill primitive that knows about the print target it lives inside*, with disclosure and IP guardrails surfaced at every step.

Anything less and the project just made it 100× faster to upload a rejectable file to MOO.
